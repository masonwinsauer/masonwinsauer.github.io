The bewildered one walks slowly. She trundles through the bright supermarket, overwhelmed by unnaturally saturated packaging, the squeals of children gleefully pulling salty and sweet snacks, being bombarded by the scent of wet meats slowly souring.

Naturally tight curls weighed and straightened by the oils of neglect anoint her shoulders as she wandered the orderly aisles. It would have been methodical if there was any premeditated reason to her path, but there was not. She had had a plan when she entered the store. She was sure of that. The numbered rows counted down - Discount easter candy? Probably not. Frozen dinners? Maybe. Coffee? While we're here. Cereal? Definitely.

The brightest lights seemed to emanate and bounce off of the white walls of the pharmacy. They pronounced the sterility and cleanliness of medical care, but they were blinding to her. She turned away, approaching at a negative angle, head bowed as the light bathed her pale greying skin.

The quickset headache begged her to leave this hostile landscape. This is where the numbers had led her, but she still did not have whatever it was that she needed, did she? The thousands of unnecessary products she had surveyed, all competing for attention hadn't overcome the thought that she was still lacking the critical element of her quest. In the search, the palace of subtle excess had swallowed her, immobilized her.

"Ma'am?"

No, no, please don't. "Please?" 

"Can I help you find something?"

"I think I'll..."

"Do you need me to call someone? Are you here with someone?"

"I think I should be able to."

She should. She should be able to do this all on her own. The fact that people were getting in the way, wanting her to do it *their* way flustered, angered her. 

"Ma'am do you need some air?"

"STOP CALLING ME THAT!"

"I'm sorry. What's your name, darlin?"

At some point she must have bent down under the weight of the oppressive norms being placed upon her. Something touched her back, just below her left scapula. It was so startling and felt so prickly that she reared back, ready to kick away whoever was applying these needle hands.

Half way up the arc of her head's journey, it encountered crunchy resistance. A wail of pain rung out. Her head bounced off of the solid object before overcorrecting and crashing into a protein chip display.

In the milliseconds that followed, her eyes were only able to make out a dark shape shrouded in red-gold halation before her feet searched for traction, finding a slippery substance that brought her back to a cracked knee. Nerves were punctured with white-hot knives as her body reacted to the impact. She kicked out again, the once slippery substance now acting as a tacky aid to her escape.

She barrelled through the one way bars that sat between her and the exit. They gave minor resistance against the momentum of her thighs. There would be bruises, but that was something to process later.

The only thought her mind allowed was "OUT".

Tires chirped and a few dainty horns voiced concern as the small girl shot from the store. Coffee and cereal this time. The next thing her brain let through was "God, what is wrong with you?"

She'd have to come back to the hateful outlet and pay for the staples. That was for another day. Coffee and cereal. We'll have to make that work.

The silky wind that moved over her face felt incredible as she ran. She closed her eyes and reveled in the static that filled her ears, a smile forming, growing, splitting. All she had to do was keep running.
