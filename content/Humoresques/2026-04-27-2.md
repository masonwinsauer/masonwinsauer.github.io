I contemplate suicide daily, hourly, minutely.

I once had a heart, a plan, a gun, a driveway, the stars.

I let the tears cut rough saline slides, tracing the outlines of tired ears. Resigned whimpers float into the ether as my wife sleeps peacefully, maddeningly oblivious in our bed. 

Could she not sense the end? 

She needn't've.

It wasn't.

Often do I think about that August night. I wonder why I spared myself. I consider what good I have done since, what harm.

A pathological liar is nothing if not predictable. Through this certainty, I have derived that the former must outweigh the latter. Sadly, I cannot feel it. I cannot parley with its truth. Regret dogs and slows my every movement, every thought. I drag the memories of a life poorly lived.

It's going to be ok. I could see you getting a little worried about me there.

You're right. I'm too hard on myself. My eyes are attuned not the light that I project, but to the wake of shadows that form from my steps.

Maybe tomorrow.
